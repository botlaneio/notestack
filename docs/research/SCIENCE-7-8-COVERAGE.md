## Scientific qualifications

Reproduction supports population continuity but is not essential to immediate individual survival. DNA copying is accurate but not perfect; variation is not always helpful or purposefully induced. Regeneration is not unrestricted ability to recreate any organism from any fragment. Potato is a modified stem and nodes/buds matter in cuttings. Tissue culture alone does not guarantee disease-free stock. Sexual reproduction does not invariably require two separate individuals (self-fertile organisms exist). Self-pollination includes transfer within the same plant; cross-pollination is between plants. Meiosis reduces chromosome sets and fertilisation restores them. Ovulation and puberty vary; ovaries do not necessarily alternate. Fertilisation and implantation occur at different usual sites; placenta normally keeps maternal/fetal blood separate while allowing exchange. Pregnancy prevention and STI protection are distinct. Health explanations are educational and support consent and appropriate care.

Human earlobe attachment and eye colour are not treated as reliable one-gene parentage tests. Dominance is not frequency or superiority. Trait-frequency age inference is conditional. Test crosses require adequate sampling. Dihybrid independence/complete dominance are assumptions, not universal rules for linked genes. Genes include functional RNA as well as protein-coding sequences. Typical X-bearing eggs and X/Y-bearing sperm give independent expected1:1 XX/XY outcomes, not a guaranteed family ratio or a complete account of sex development/gender. Neither parent chooses the outcome. Source ABO exercise distinguishes what one observation establishes from what established ABO knowledge adds. The dog-coat project uses existing data or simulations rather than arranging animal breeding.
