## Mathematical decisions and source conventions

- Sector versus segment and minor versus major are explicitly distinguished. Arc length, sector area and sector perimeter use their proper dimensions. The 60°,90°,120°central-triangle areas are derived.
- Unless overridden, exercises use π=22/7. Chapter 11 questions4,6,7,8 and 12 use 3.14 as stated. Questions 6 and 7 use sqrt(3)=1.73; the table-cover design uses sqrt(3)=1.7. The table-cover figure was inspected in the rendered PDF: the 6 designs are the minor segments outside an inscribed regular hexagon.
- Exact fractions or surds are requested where no final precision is stated. Chapter 12 Exercise 12.1 8 asks for nearest cm² (18 from 17.6). The syrup estimate is explicitly requested to nearest cm³ (338 from 338.184), and the pen-stand volume to 2 decimal places (523.53). Models retain unrounded intermediate values.
- The playing-top example explains the book’s l≈3.7, area≈39.6 convention alongside the unrounded-slant calculation≈39.55. The bird-bath example explicitly follows the book’s ground-supported interpretation, excluding its bottom.
- Surface models trace exposed boundary, not sums of closed total areas: hidden contact discs, exposed annuli, missing lids, grounded bases and curved cavity walls are identified. Volume models add non-overlapping pieces or subtract cavities/obstructions/displaced solids.
- Capsules subtract 2r from entire length for the cylindrical part; two-cone models subtract both cone heights. The source “in terms of π” question requests the coefficient of π rather than forcing an approximation.
- Mass conversion uses density in g/cm³ then converts grams to kg. The sphere-plus-neck capacity model yields≈346.51 cm³, not 345. Capacity uses inside dimensions.
