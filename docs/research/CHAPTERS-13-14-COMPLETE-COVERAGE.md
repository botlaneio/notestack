## Source and mathematical decisions

- All Statistics question tables are included as accessible, horizontally scrollable tables. Raw marks, grouped marks, less-than cumulative data and missing frequencies are represented separately rather than silently converted into one generic distribution.
- Direct, assumed-mean and step-deviation methods use the same weighted class marks. Unequal class widths do not invalidate the mean algebra. The midpoint assumption and difference between a grouped estimate and an exact raw-data mean are explicit.
- Mode models identify the lower boundary, width and neighbouring frequencies. The lab does not fabricate a unique mode for tied peaks or apply the chapter’s equal-width mode formula to unequal-width data.
- Median models distinguish ordinary frequency f from preceding cumulative frequency cf, retain inclusive-to-continuous boundary adjustments and recover frequencies by differences. The first lower boundary of Example 7 is not invented; its median lies in a later known class.
- All numeric estimates specify final precision. Standard rounding of 35.375 gives 35.38; the source answer list prints 35.37. In Exercise 13.3 1, exact mean 2330/17 and mode 1765/13 round to 137.06 and 135.77, while the source prints 137.05 and 135.76. The models show these differences rather than marking a correct standard rounding as wrong. SO₂ mean uses 3 decimal places.
- The empirical 3Median≈Mode+2Mean relation is labelled approximate, not a universal identity. Grouped fractional mode/median values are estimates, not necessarily actual observed values.
- Probability counts equally likely elementary outcomes, not category names, cash values or distinct repeated-face labels. Ordered pairs and ordered toss sequences are explicitly retained; removal without replacement changes the next denominator.
- Fair-coin, fair-die, uniform-draw, independent-birthday and uniform time/area models are stated. The source’s random true/false guess and idealised birth categories are explained as model assumptions; two named possibilities alone do not imply equal likelihood.
- The birthday-complement exercise uses its supplied 0.992 event probability without substituting a different birthday experiment. Additional Probability Examples 10/11 and Exercise 14.1 20 keep the book’s “not for examination” label. Continuous probabilities use length or area ratios; the circular-region answer stays π/24, represented by its coefficient in a typed field.

## Learning and assessment

Both final chapters use the same guided, textbook-practice, independent-check and source-map modes. Typed numbers are checked using the existing restricted arithmetic parser. Written arguments, interpretations and model justifications remain saved self-review, not automatic proof grading. Teacher verification remains pending. Existing Chapters 1–12 IDs and storage keys are preserved.

Interactive labs let learners vary frequencies, change the mean method/assumed mean/scale, inspect cumulative counts, and compare tied or unequal-width cases. Probability labs enumerate all 36 ordered dice pairs and 4/8 coin sequences, highlight event subsets, and contrast fair-coin theory with observed finite-trial proportions. Experimental outcomes are not required to match the theoretical probability exactly.
