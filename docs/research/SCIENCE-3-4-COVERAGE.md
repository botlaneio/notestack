## Scientific/source clarifications

Physical trends have exceptions:mercury,bromine,iodine,graphite,diamond,soft alkali metals and low-melting Ga/Cs. Classification is not a dangerous handling/cutting task. Reactivity comparisons depend on reagent,water,films and conditions;nitric-acid behaviour includes the source very-dilute Mg/Mn exception. Molten-salt Na extraction is distinguished from aqueous brine electrolysis. Ores are economically extractable materials,not every metal-bearing mineral. High-temperature extraction,thermite,mercury compounds and aqua regia are explained conceptually,without practical preparation instructions.

The source frying-pan zinc MCQ's intended answer is separated from real food-contact suitability. The hot-water-tank comparison does not claim every steel is unsuitable. Stainless is corrosion-resistant,not universally immune;alloy properties and phases are not universally described by one rule. Zinc sacrifice needs remaining Zn and electrical contact.

Carbon Lewis models distinguish sharing from transfer and weak intermolecular attractions from strong intramolecular bonds. Graphite/benzene delocalisation clarifies simplified fixed-bond drawings. CnH2n does not prove an alkene;the formula families state acyclic/multiple-bond assumptions. Ketones need at least3 carbons;locants/carbonyl carbon counting are explicit. Bromopentane's three straight-chain positions are not claimed as all constitutional isomers. A catalyst differs from a consumed oxidising agent. Alkaline alcohol oxidation gives carboxylate before acid work-up;the chapter acid equation is labelled a shorthand. Esterification is reversible;small ester hydrolysis does not make useful long-chain soap. Ethanol/industrial alcohol/oxidants and flames are not home procedures or consumption advice. The atmospheric0.03% CO2 and short silicon-chain examples are not asserted as present-day universal limits. Micelles are orientation models with conditions,not measurements;foam alone is not a quantitative cleanliness/hardness measure.
